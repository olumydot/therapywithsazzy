Children

Helping children understand what they feel.

We help children understand what they feel and develop skills to manage what they experience, in ways that feel safe, playful, and respectful.

Big feelings can be difficult to understand. You can learn to manage them.

What we can work on

Common areas of support

Emotional regulation

Naming feelings, noticing the body’s signals, and practicing calm-down strategies that actually work for your child.

Frustration tolerance

Building the skills to handle disappointment, waiting, and “no” without meltdowns.

Coping skills

A toolbox of simple, age-appropriate strategies children can use at home and at school.

Self-esteem & confidence

Helping children see their strengths and feel capable.

Transitions

Support through school changes, family changes, and new routines.

Behavioral challenges

Understanding the need underneath behavior, together with parents.

ADHD, autism & learning differences

Respectful, individualized support for the emotional side of neurodivergence and learning-related challenges.

Approach

Your child is more than their diagnosis.

Different minds may need different approaches. Support should recognize the individual, not just the diagnosis. The goal is never to change who a child is. It’s to give them tools, language, and confidence.

  • Child-friendly language and activities
  • Parents included as partners
  • Strengths-based and neurodiversity-informed
  • Clear information on consent and confidentiality for minors
About Sazzy
Questions

What people ask

How is support for a child different from adult support?

Children learn best through play, routines, and relationships. Support uses age-appropriate language and activities, and involves parents so new skills carry over at home.

Does my child need a diagnosis?

No. Many families reach out simply because a child is struggling with big feelings. Support focuses on skills and needs rather than labels.

What about consent and privacy for minors?

A parent or guardian’s consent is required. Confidentiality, and its limits, are explained clearly to parents and to children in age-appropriate ways.

You don’t have to figure everything out alone.

Reach out to ask a question, learn about next steps, or inquire about a workshop. There’s no pressure and no obligation.
